Chapter 04 · Hardware track

Make motion
dependable.

Your first useful control system turns a small command into measured wheel movement—and stops when the command disappears.

The unpowered chassis tells us the parts fit. This chapter adds energy and feedback. We will bring up one wheel with your Windows computer, verify its encoder, repeat for the second wheel, and establish a bounded manual drive interface. Mapping comes after that interface can explain what the wheels actually did.

An encoder reports rotation; it cannot prove the tyre travelled the same distance along the floor. A velocity controller corrects wheel-speed error; it cannot notice a stair. Keeping those boundaries clear makes faults easier to locate. When the robot bends away from a straight line, we can ask separately whether its commands, wheel speeds, or traction differed.

Build status: the component interfaces below are supported by manufacturer documentation. This combined rover has not been assembled or physically tested for the book. The manufacturer software supplies the first-wheel bring-up. A ROS integration candidate and acceptance contract are supplied for the next stage; a validated, complete survey-rover driver/configuration is not bundled.

The reference electronics

Part or interfaceDefault and reason
Two drive motorsPololu #4885 LP 12 V encoder motors from Chapter 02. The nominal encoder specification is approximately 2,249 quadrature counts per output revolution; verify the controller's actual counting convention.
Local motor controlBasicmicro RoboClaw 2x7A, IMC404. It combines two motor channels, encoder inputs, local velocity feedback, and USB communication. Its current capability exceeds these motors' requirements; motor protection still needs configuration.
Motor powerA commercially assembled eight-cell NiMH pack, nominal 9.6 V, with a matched charger and documented current/connector rating. Verify its charged voltage is no more than 12 V before connecting this motor pair. Lower-than-nominal motor voltage reduces available performance; measure it on the real rover.
Power pathA fuse near battery positive, a reachable DC-rated positive-line disconnect, and properly terminated wiring. A 3 A fuse is a starting wiring-protection candidate only when the pack, connectors, switch, and wire are all rated appropriately; finalize its type and trip behaviour against the actual parts.
Robot computerA modest x86 mini-PC running native Ubuntu 24.04, with USB ports for controller and sensors. Verify Linux, USB, cooling, and supply compatibility before purchase. Use its manufacturer's supported power arrangement; the RoboClaw's 5 V, 800 mA output is not a PC supply.
Bench computer and connectionYour Windows 11 computer runs Motion Studio for initial setup. Use the manufacturer's USB isolation guidance, especially when motor and computer power have separate ground paths.

Keep motor power and computer power as two explicitly documented systems. A USB connector does not make their positive rails interchangeable. The build sheet records the voltage range, startup demand, connector polarity, and source for every item. Buy the drivetrain first; confirm the computer and sensor power arrangements before adding their weight to the deck.

Three electrical ideas for this build: voltage is the electrical supply condition across a component; current is the flow it draws under the present load. A motor usually draws more current as resisting torque increases. A stall is a powered motor whose shaft cannot turn, a condition that can heat it rapidly. For example, a 12 V supply capable of 7 A does not force 7 A through a motor; the motor and load determine the draw, and faults can demand more than small wiring or motors can tolerate. This is why we check both voltage and current limits before applying power.

Session · Draw the power path before wiring it

45–60 minutes · Battery disconnected · Output: a labelled wiring diagram and checked harness

Draw battery positive → fuse → disconnect → controller positive. Draw battery negative directly to controller negative. Then draw the motor outputs and the separate encoder supply. Use the controller's operational requirements to check the actual board revision, ratings, and disconnect arrangement. Remove positive power for a controlled shutdown; breaking only battery negative while USB is attached can create an unintended return path.

  1. With the pack unplugged, assemble the fuse holder, switch, and keyed connector. Use terminals suited to the actual screw terminals and conductor sizes; keep loose strands and unsupported splices away from the board. Secure the harness so pulling a cable does not pull its electrical contact.
  2. Use continuity mode only on the unpowered harness. Confirm each intended path and check that positive and negative are not shorted. A brief charging indication across controller capacitors is different from a persistent harness short; isolate the harness if the reading is unclear.
  3. Put the meter into DC voltage mode, with its leads in the voltage/common sockets. Measure the disconnected pack's polarity and voltage. A negative reading with the red probe on the intended positive contact means the connector or probe assignment needs correction. Do not place a current-mode meter across a battery.
  4. Record the pack's measured charged voltage and manufacturer discharge limits. Set controller voltage limits from that pack's documentation; use custom settings for the selected NiMH pack rather than an unrelated lithium-cell preset.
  5. Keep the wheels clear of the table on a stable stand. Leave both motor outputs disconnected for the first controller connection.

A regenerative controller can return energy from a slowing motor to its supply. A generic wall adapter may not absorb it. The chosen pack and controller limits must accommodate this behaviour; using a bench supply requires the manufacturer's documented clamp arrangement. This is why substituting a convenient adapter is a design change, even when its voltage label looks right.

Done: you can trace both supply conductors, identify the fuse and disconnect, and show the measured polarity before any motor turns.

Session · Connect one motor and read its encoder

45–60 minutes · Windows 11 and Motion Studio · Output: a verified direction and count

Disconnect power before wiring. For the specified Pololu motor, red and black are the motor pair. Green is encoder ground, blue is encoder supply, and yellow/white are channels A/B. Confirm those assignments against the exact motor page; colours from another motor are not an electrical standard. Supply this encoder from the controller's documented 5 V and ground pins, not the motor output.

Left motor connectionController destination
Red / black motor wiresM1A / M1B; adjust direction only with power removed or through documented configuration.
Green / blue encoder wiresEncoder ground / 5 V.
Yellow / white encoder signalsChannel 1 encoder A / B, using the board's labelled pinout.

The right motor will use M2A/M2B and encoder channel 2. Treat both motors as separate systems until each passes. Loose breadboard contacts are unsuitable for the motor-power path. Keep the encoder leads secured away from moving wheels and avoid routing a long loose bundle alongside the motor wiring.

  1. Install Motion Studio from the manufacturer downloads page. Power the controller from its main supply before connecting USB, then confirm its reported model, firmware, and voltage. Save those versions.
  2. Keep the output command at zero and turn the wheel slowly by hand. Observe whether the encoder changes. Mark the tyre and measure one output revolution; expect approximately 2,249 counts for the stated quadrature convention. Gear backlash and stopping exactly on the mark limit this hand check.
  3. Enable only channel 1 for a brief low-duty command with the wheel suspended. Observe direction and count sign. Define positive as the wheel motion that would drive the chassis forward. Verify the other direction, then command zero.
  4. If positive motion produces negative counts, correct the documented encoder direction setting before enabling velocity feedback. A feedback loop with the wrong sign can increase its command while trying to fix the apparent error.
  5. Power down, wire the second motor, and repeat. Label the cable groups LEFT and RIGHT and record the final motor/encoder direction configuration.

Use the manufacturer's quick-start guide to locate the controls on your installed version. USB supplies communication here; it is not the controller's main power source.

Done: each positive command and count agrees with your forward convention, turning one wheel changes only its corresponding encoder, and zero halts each wheel. Fill in the drive check log.

Session · Command a speed, then compare it with the measurement

One or more 45–60 minute sessions · Wheels supported first · Output: a low-speed response plot

The controller's PID loop adjusts motor output to reduce the difference between requested and measured encoder speed. Its parameters describe this motor, supply, and load. Follow Velocity Settings and the current tuning procedure for your revision. Automatic tuning can cause substantial motion; keep wheels supported, secure the chassis, and verify current and travel assumptions before invoking it.

The motor's extrapolated stall current at 12 V is 0.9 A. That is not a continuous operating target. Pololu's general continuous-current guidance is 25% of stall current or less, which is 0.225 A for this example. Begin with brief, unloaded low-output checks and a conservative current limit appropriate to the motor. Verify the controller can enforce and report the chosen limit; do not assume a high-current controller's sensing resolves small currents accurately enough without checking. If continuous loaded operation demands more current, reduce drag or payload and revisit the motor selection.

Plot the requested speed alongside measured speed during a small step and a return to zero. Start with a target corresponding to approximately 0.05 m/s at the floor, then inspect overshoot, settling, and current. Using an illustrative 80 mm rolling diameter, one revolution covers about 0.251 m; 0.05 m/s corresponds to roughly 447 encoder counts per second. The real rolling diameter and counted edges determine the final conversion.

Make the requested-versus-measured plot

Open Velocity Settings in Motion Studio. Use one graph channel for Motor 1 Setpoint and another for Motor 1 iSpeed, following the manufacturer's graphing guide. The selected screen gives the setpoint its meaning; here it is speed. The ordinary Speed trace is averaged, while iSpeed reports instantaneous encoder speed, so record which signal you use.

  1. Keep channel 2 at zero. Clear the graph, select a small slider range, and show a brief zero → low speed → zero sequence for channel 1 with the wheel supported.
  2. Scale the traces so their numeric axes are readable. Compare requested and measured values using those axes; independently scaled lines can appear to overlap even when their values differ.
  3. Save a screenshot containing the channel names, scale, and return to zero. Use Save CSV, documented in the Velocity Settings reference, to save the original data as runs/drive-left-original.csv. Record the software version, units, and signal names beside it; preserve the exported headers.
  4. Open the CSV and identify its time and channel columns before making an XY plot. If your installed version lacks export or an explicit timebase, keep the labelled screenshot as this checkpoint; do not invent timestamps. Repeat separately for channel 2 and save a second result.

The documented display update is 100 ms, so this view cannot establish sub-sample response time. Identify a visible overshoot, persistent oscillation, steady offset, or clean return to zero; report only what the recorded resolution supports. This procedure uses the existing controller graph and does not require a new plotting program.

Run the same experiment on each wheel. A wheel that reports the right speed in the air may still stall when loaded. On the floor, begin in a clear bounded area with stair access physically blocked and the disconnect within reach. Keep pets and children outside this initial test area. Start and stop gently; a polished floor makes traction another variable to measure.

Done: both wheels follow small requests without sustained oscillation, return to zero, and operate within your recorded motor/current limits. Save the plot and the controller configuration. A failure here belongs to the drivetrain; do not try to hide it by adjusting SLAM.

Setup gate · Prepare the robot computer before the ROS bridge

One or more setup blocks · Native Ubuntu 24.04 · Result: two ordinary ROS programs exchange messages

The remaining controller work uses ROS, so establish its environment here. On the selected robot computer, use native Ubuntu 24.04 and follow the official Jazzy Ubuntu installation through its Desktop Install option. The official source version is a fallback if that page does not render. This is the Ubuntu shell, outside the model-training virtual environment.

After completing the repository and package instructions, run these commands in terminal A. The first two create a local project folder on the Ubuntu computer and enter it:

mkdir -p ~/home-rover/notes ~/home-rover/runs
cd ~/home-rover
source /opt/ros/jazzy/setup.bash
sudo apt install ros-jazzy-tf2-tools
ros2 run demo_nodes_cpp talker

In a second Ubuntu terminal, run:

source /opt/ros/jazzy/setup.bash
ros2 run demo_nodes_py listener

The expected result is a publishing message in A and corresponding received messages in B. Stop both with Ctrl+C. Save the Ubuntu release, ROS distribution and package versions in your project notes. Keep motor outputs disabled during software setup.

If the demo fails: first check that both terminals sourced the same Jazzy installation. If a demo package is missing, return to the Desktop Install step. Diagnose this local software exchange before adding USB devices, networking, or a controller driver.

Done: the local example exchanges messages. Continue directly to the bounded controller interface below; Chapter 05 reuses this installation.

Session · Give the rover a bounded software interface

Several integration sessions · Ubuntu robot computer · Output: manual movement with measured feedback and tested timeouts

The robot computer converts a body velocity request into two wheel speeds and publishes measurements back. Use the manufacturer's ROS 2 driver as an integration candidate, with its Python library available for smaller interface experiments. Pin the source revision you inspect. The documentation's example dimensions, commands, and installer defaults are not this robot's configuration; review them before use on the Jazzy system.

First establish a read-only connection: query firmware and encoder counts with motor commands disabled. Confirm your serial device by its identity, not by assuming it will always be /dev/ttyACM0. Then configure measured wheel radius, track width, count convention, signs, current limits, and maximum velocity. Keep the first linear speed cap at 0.10 m/s and initial trials at 0.05 m/s. These are conservative starting limits for evaluation, not proven safe speeds for every floor or collision.

Interface contractEvidence required before navigation
Fresh body commandOnly forward velocity and yaw rate are accepted for this base. Reject non-finite values, clamp limits, and expire the request after 0.25 s without a fresh operator command.
Controller communicationSet a 0.5 s serial timeout, save it, power-cycle, and read it back. Keep host-command expiry separate: continuing encoder queries must not preserve an old velocity request.
Measured stateRead encoder differences with timestamps; handle wraparound and controller reset explicitly. Invalid reads or reset trigger a stop and a logged fault, not a fabricated distance.
ROS integrationMatch the installed navigation stack's actual command type, including Twist versus TwistStamped. Publish wheel-derived odometry and exactly one odom→base_link transform owner. Chapter 06 develops the geometry.
Restart behaviourStart disarmed at zero. After a reset, communication fault, or physical stop, require a deliberate re-arm and a fresh operator command. Reconnecting alone or receiving an old stream does not restore motion.

The controller settings reference documents the communication timeout. Treat the specified 0.25/0.5 s values as initial design settings and verify their behaviour on your installed firmware and driver. A delayed process or an unhealthy connection is part of the experiment, not something we assume away.

Ask your coding agent to inspect the pinned driver's implementation against this table and implement only missing behaviour. Have it identify where command age is checked, where a failed encoder read is handled, and where zero is sent. You should be able to trace one request from input to wheel command and one measurement back to odometry before accepting the change.

Done: first with wheels suspended, stop the velocity publisher while encoder polling continues; then kill the bridge process; then disconnect its network input. Test the physical disconnect separately. Record whether and when motion ceases in every case. Repeat on the bounded floor at the initial speed and record stopping distance. Resolve any continued or resumed movement before attaching navigation.

When the first movement disagrees with your prediction

SymptomNext diagnostic
No encoder movementCheck encoder supply/reference and channel assignments with power removed before changing wiring. Verify a hand turn changes the expected channel.
Wheel accelerates in velocity modeDisconnect motor power. Recheck command/encoder signs and count units with low-duty open-loop tests.
Computer resets when motors startMeasure power under load, inspect the separated supply paths and USB grounding, and check startup demand. More training data cannot fix a voltage drop.
Robot curves while both speed plots agreeInspect rolling wheel diameter, tyre slip, caster drag, and load distribution. Equal angular speed does not guarantee equal ground travel.
Robot continues after the operator stopsTest host command expiry while status polling remains active. Then test the independent controller timeout by terminating communication entirely.
