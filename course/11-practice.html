a way back in.

A useful plan tells you what to do when things work, when they fail, and when you return after a week away.

The exciting part is the robot that understands your home. Today’s work might be measuring a bracket or finding a mislabeled photograph. Your workbench connects that task to the capability it supports and keeps your next action beside the last evidence you saved. Start there when you sit down to build. Use this chapter when you want the full route, the recovery approach, or a backup of your notes.

A focused session gives you a purpose, materials, ordered actions and a finish condition. Its full bench instructions stay available with the required setup and safety information. You can open the chapter for more context or follow the one relevant resource without losing the session you intend to resume.

The first rover has a finish line

Your core project is complete when you can show the fitted assembly, dependable manual motion and stop tests, a checked room map, a short navigation demonstration, an honest model evaluation, and those five real linked observations. The synthetic memory exercise teaches the contract; the real records show that your model and robot data actually meet.

The workbench groups session checks into capabilities and keeps your evidence visible. Chapter checkmarks remain a separate reference record for the ten core chapters, 00–09. A saved experiment can document useful progress even when a check has not passed; only mark a session complete when you can support its finish condition. Chapter 10 is optional research and this project desk is a utility chapter. Neither adds work to the core finish line.

Optional next project · Reconstruct a small part of the room in 3D

Once the core evidence is saved, use Chapter 10's reconstruction experiment to investigate a recorded RGB-D scene. Begin with one corner and compare measured geometry. This is a new experiment with its own entry and exit checks; it does not change whether your first rover project is complete.

The controller bridge, sensor integration and navigation configuration are substantial software projects inside the course. Their interface and acceptance checks are specified; you will still implement and verify them with your hardware. At each integration session, inspect the stated uncertainty and use the smallest listed check to investigate it. Budget several focused sessions for those integrations. A named unknown gives us an experiment to perform; its result tells us whether to continue, adjust a part or revise the plan.

When a step stops moving

Open the focused session’s I’m stuck help for a diagnostic matched to the current task. If a print or delivery is holding you up, use Waiting for parts to find an available task on the other track. The suggestion depends on the checks you have recorded; inspect its requirements before starting. Keep the unfinished session and its next action saved so you can return when the part arrives.

Use agents to accelerate work you can explain

An agent can write a dataset checker or adapt a controller interface quickly. Your learning comes from specifying the observable behavior, predicting a result, and investigating the mismatch. Before accepting a change, explain its inputs, outputs, failure behavior and evidence in your own words. You do not need to type every line to own the experiment.

For a hardware integration, give the agent an interface with measured facts. This is a prompt template, not a motor-control program:

Task: implement one acceptance row from our controller contract.
Inputs: measured wheel geometry, verified count convention,
        exact controller protocol/version, chosen command type.
Behavior: bounded velocity requests; zero on stale commands;
          explicit re-arm after stop; timestamped encoder telemetry.
Deliver: a small diff, mock I/O checks, and an explanation of
         which claims still require a wheels-off-ground bench test.
Do not invent missing pinouts, current ratings, or calibration values.
First explain what evidence would show this row is satisfied.

For the model, ask for a review of split independence, preprocessing, checkpoint selection and the meaning of the metric. Have a second agent inspect the experiment contract before you accept an unexpectedly excellent result. Keep data and code versions with every report so that a later improvement has a fair comparison.

What this edition supplies

The starter downloads include working desktop programs for training an image classifier and querying observation memory, their synthetic checks, practical fit coupons, and measurement worksheets. Their execution evidence is limited to the checks described in the lessons. Synthetic patterns are useful for exercising a pipeline; they do not demonstrate household perception.

The full rover, power system, printed chassis, ROS controller integration, live SLAM and Nav2 behavior require your measured components and physical verification. The book provides a reference architecture and staged acceptance tasks for that work. It does not claim that an unbuilt robot has completed a trip, achieved a measurement accuracy, or recognized your belongings.

A fresh course, with the earlier work preserved. The previous edition contains the original rover, arm, articulated creature and simulated joint experiments. Its browser notes remain separate. This edition starts a new progress record so old checkmarks cannot silently complete different lessons.
