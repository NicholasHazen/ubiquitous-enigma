Resources for your next experiment.

Books, videos, audio conversations, and technical references for the home survey rover and its first trained models.

We’re building a rover that measures a room and remembers what it saw. You already know how to program; this shelf supports the newer skills: fitting parts, powering motion, reading sensors, training a model, and connecting an observation to a place. Each recommendation gives you a small piece to read, watch, or listen to, something to notice, and a link back to your build.

If you want a book beside the bench

For the first physical build, I’d choose Making Things Move. For electrical experiments, use Make: Electronics when a measurement needs more explanation. For training, the free Python edition of An Introduction to Statistical Learning is the main book. Paid books are optional; the free references and supplied exercises support the default route.

Keep the vision in view

We’re working toward a rover you can ask about a room: where it can travel, what it observed, and what evidence supports its answer. Join the tracks: remember five real observations

Your next resource is the supplied observation-memory walkthrough and starter. Work through its small synthetic example, then open Session 09B to import five observations from your own model and mounted camera. Keep the image, capture time, model identity, score meaning, map revision and capture pose together.

Finish this step when: a query returns those real records and you can show the evidence for each answer. Here, the capture pose records the rover’s base_link pose when the image was taken; it does not establish the toy’s position. Read depth projection only when you have the measurements needed to calculate an object location. You can complete the core course before adding a scene graph or an LLM.

Later research · Choose one question after the core build

For your first extension, follow reconstruct one corner with recorded RGB-D and known camera poses. After that, choose the question you want to answer: which volume is observed free?, how does the room look from another viewpoint?, which objects relate to each other?, or how can language select a working robot skill? These are separate experiments. Keep one question, one recorded dataset and one acceptance check per detour.
