04 · First drive

Put the wheels
on the floor

Your printed deck becomes a machine that can move, turn and stop. A short, slightly crooked drive is enough to start.

The program now understands our little vocabulary. Your rolling chassis is already assembled. We will inspect it with the electronics in place, check one wheel at a time in the air, then try a few short floor movements. The camera stays in its box until the wheels behave.

On the bench · two sessions of about 35 minutes

Bring: your assembled Chapter 01 rolling chassis and spare caster spacers; wired Uno and shield with rover.ino loaded; charged pack; both motors; two TT wheels #3757; caster #3949; cable ties; thin foam; insulating mounting pads; M3 bolts, nuts and washers; screwdriver; ruler; masking tape; and a stable nonconductive block to raise the wheels.

Finish line: forward moves the rover forward, left turns it left, and a single command expires on its own. We will use a clear floor, not a tabletop.

04A · Check the rolling chassis with power disconnected

Keep the motors, wheels and caster you assembled in Chapter 01 in place. This is a final inspection, not a rebuild. We are adding the electronics and checking that their weight has not introduced rubbing or wobble.

  1. Switch off the pack and unplug USB. Confirm that the wide end of the T-shaped deck is the front and the narrower 90 mm end holds the motors. Both wheel axles belong on the line 35 mm forward of the rear edge. The metal motor cans point toward the front.
  2. Check each motor's two cable ties. Each tie runs through its inner slot, around the outside edge of the narrow deck and around the motor body. The inner slots are at X = ±25 mm and Y = −58 / −15 mm in the Chapter 01 drawing; there are no outer tie holes. Slide the ties within their slots as needed to clear shafts and terminals. The foam beneath each body should prevent rocking without letting the axle tilt.
  3. Inspect each #3757 wheel and its gently tightened retaining screw. Keep at least 2 mm clearance between tire and deck. Nothing should scrape when the wheel turns; do not force a stiff gearbox by hand.
  4. Keep the caster spacer stack you fitted in Chapter 01. It was chosen by measuring the deck-to-floor gap with the drive wheels touching and subtracting the caster's 16 mm height. Check that its M3 bolts enter from the caster side, with washers and nuts above the deck. Spare thread should point upward, away from the floor.
  5. Strap the battery holder on top near the center, ahead of the drive axle. Keep its switch accessible. Mount the Uno/shield on insulating pads with removable mounting tape; its underside must not rest on metal hardware. Keep the USB socket accessible. Tie wires to the deck so pulling a cable cannot pull its terminal loose.
  6. Set the loaded chassis on a flat surface. Measure from the surface to the deck's underside near the front and rear. If the front is lower, add the matching 1 or 2 mm spacer; if higher, remove that amount. All three contact points should touch. Recheck wheel clearance and tighten the caster nuts once the deck is level.

These are workshop prototype parts. Their geometry has been checked in software, but their physical fit has not been tested for this edition. If a printed opening binds, fix that fit with power off. The zip ties and removable pads are deliberate: you should be able to inspect and change the machine without dismantling a decorative shell.

04B · Establish what “forward” means

Place a stable block beneath the deck so both wheels are clear of the bench. The block must support the chassis without touching exposed electronics. Add a small tape mark to each tire so you can see which way it rotates.

  1. Check the VIN jumper is still removed. Keep the pack off, connect USB, open Serial Monitor at 115200 baud, and press reset. Wait for READY.
  2. Switch motor power on. Nothing should move. If a wheel starts without a command, switch off and return to the Chapter 03 upload and startup checks.
  3. Send 1 once. Only the left wheel should turn briefly, then coast to rest. At the top of that wheel, the tire should travel toward the robot's front; that rotation would propel the chassis forward.
  4. If the left wheel turns the other way, switch the pack off, unplug USB and swap that motor's two wires at M1. Reconnect, wait for READY, switch on and repeat 1. Do not change code and wiring at the same time.
  5. Send 2 and perform the same check for the right wheel at M2. The motors face opposite sides, so their wire orders need not match. What matters is that both have the same meaning of forward.
  6. Send F, wait for stop, then send B. Both wheels should agree in each case. Send L: left wheel backward, right wheel forward. Send R: the opposite. Send X to finish.

A stop test you can see

With the wheels raised, send F once and then take your hands off the keyboard. The motor command should be released after about 350 ms and the wheels should coast to rest. Watch the wheel as well as STOP timeout. Repeat this three times. Do not proceed to the floor if any run keeps driving.

Next send FX in one Serial Monitor submission. The final command is stop, so the sketch should finish that input batch stopped rather than hold forward. This checks the stop command; the silence test above checks the timeout. Later, the camera script will let us interrupt a continuous stream.

04C · Check the pack under this small load

Turn the pack off. With the multimeter still in DC-voltage mode, secure its probes or insulated test clips across the motor-power + and − terminals, with no exposed metal able to bridge them. Keep leads clear of the wheels. Switch on and send short F commands, reading the voltage while the wheels turn. If it drops below 4.5 V, switch off and recharge the NiMH cells before continuing. If you cannot secure the probes without holding them near moving parts, turn off and use insulated clips; do not juggle loose probes and a moving robot.

The meter may miss a brief dip; this is a useful check, not a complete picture of electrical noise. Remove the probes with power off before the floor run.

04D · Make a tiny floor journey

  1. Clear a roughly one-meter square of smooth, level floor. Keep fingers, loose cables and pets outside it. Place the computer where a short rover movement cannot pull it off a surface. Lay the USB cable behind the rover with slack and keep it away from both wheels.
  2. Put a tape line across the floor and place the front of the rover behind it. Keep one hand near the pack switch.
  3. Send one F. Let the rover stop. Send a second F, then a third, checking the cable between pulses. You have just driven your printed machine using a high-level request.
  4. Send one L, let it stop, then one R. If it only buzzes, switch off promptly and use the table below. If left and right are reversed, return to the raised-wheel M1/M2 and direction checks.
  5. Switch the pack off. Write down which way it curved and whether either wheel rubbed. Photograph the assembled rover from above so you can recreate the layout.

The two-wheel arrangement is called differential drive. It steers by making its wheels move differently. Our first program requests equal electrical effort, so a straight-looking command may still draw a curve on the floor. The next chapter gives that curve a useful job: it becomes something we can measure.

If your rover has opinions

What happenedYour next action
Serial messages appear; neither motor movesSwitch off, check the motor-power indicator and pack voltage, then check the M1/M2 wires are in motor-terminal pairs rather than their neighboring GND terminal.
One motor moves; the other does notSwitch off and unplug USB. Check the silent motor's terminal grip and wheel clearance. Repeat its single-wheel command on the stand before changing code.
A wheel buzzes or barely twitchesSwitch off immediately. Check rubbing, motor alignment and charged-pack voltage. If freely mounted wheels still do not start on the stand at 90, change DRIVE_PWM to 110, upload with pack off, and repeat the stand checks. Do not keep increasing it to force a jam.
Straight pulses work; floor turns buzzFirst check that the caster rolls and all wheels clear the deck. Then change only TURN_PWM from 70 to 90, upload with pack off and repeat raised-wheel checks before another floor turn.
The Uno resets when motors startStop and inspect the removed VIN jumper, pack charge, wiring and header joints. Keep USB and motor power separate. Do not hide the reset by extending the timeout.
It arcs consistentlyCheck axle alignment and rubbing, then keep the observation. Do not buy encoders or rewrite the controller yet; Chapter 05 starts from this result.

Done for today

Your rover has moved forward, reversed, turned both ways, and stopped after a single command expired. Save the exact sketch that passed, including any PWM changes. Put a small label on the chassis: M1 = left, M2 = right.
